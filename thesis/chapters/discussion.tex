\chapter{Discussion}
\label{chap:discussion}

\section{What the Results Support}

The strongest supported claim is that dataset-aware configuration improves the tested standard
AdaLoRA baseline when enough data is available. This claim appears in both major experiment
archives. The improvement is not merely a tiny fluctuation at 5,000 examples: it is 4.97 points in
the primary experiment and 12.68 points in the separate five-seed archive.

The selected-rank trend also validates a central mechanical goal. A 100-example subset does not
receive the same target rank as a 10,000-example subset. Star-LoRA thereby removes one fixed
assumption from standard AdaLoRA. It also produces a record of why a setting was selected, which
is useful for debugging and further research.

\section{What the Results Do Not Support}

The experiments do not show that Star-LoRA universally outperforms LoRA. Fixed-rank LoRA is the
best PEFT method at several budgets and is dramatically faster. Nor do the experiments show that
the stability score improves allocation, because the score is not connected to the allocator.

The results also do not isolate rank adaptation from module expansion. Star-LoRA changes several
variables simultaneously. Its 5,000-example performance may come from a higher target rank, larger
initial rank, different scaling, different dropout, a different schedule, expanded attention
targets, expanded MLP targets, or interactions among them.

This distinction changes how the work should be presented. The contribution is not a proven
optimal rank formula. It is a prototype and empirical investigation showing that data-conditioned
configuration can improve AdaLoRA, together with evidence about the conditions under which the
current rules are costly or ineffective.

\section{Why Star-LoRA May Help AdaLoRA}

Three explanations are plausible. First, target rank 8 may be too rigid across budgets. Star-LoRA
reduces rank on small datasets and increases it on larger datasets. Second, the longer allocation
schedule may give the model more time before the final budget is fixed. Third, additional
projections may carry useful task capacity that standard AdaLoRA cannot access.

The current experiment cannot determine the relative contribution of these explanations. A
factorial ablation is required. It is possible, for example, that module expansion explains most
of the accuracy gain while rank selection contributes little. It is equally possible that the
longer schedule protects useful rank components from early pruning.

\section{Why Fixed LoRA Remains Strong}

LoRA avoids dynamic pruning during short training. Every selected low-rank direction remains
available for all epochs, and the optimizer does not need to estimate a structural allocation from
early gradients. Rank 8 also appears to be a strong setting for Qwen2.5-0.5B on SST-2.

Furthermore, LoRA targets only query and value projections in this implementation. Its smaller
adapter surface may act as effective regularization and produces much higher throughput. The
comparison illustrates a general research lesson: an adaptive method must be evaluated against a
simple baseline that receives an appropriate rank and learning rate.

The result also provides one answer to the question of why AdaLoRA may be less attractive in
practice. When a fixed-rank baseline is both faster and highly accurate, the user has little reason
to accept a more complicated schedule unless adaptive allocation provides a consistent or
budget-specific advantage.

\section{Extreme Low-Resource Failure}

At 100 examples, all PEFT methods in the primary sweep are near chance. Reducing Star-LoRA's target
rank does not solve the problem. Several factors may contribute:
\begin{itemize}
  \item only 39 optimization steps are estimated over three epochs;
  \item the sequence-classification head begins with little task information;
  \item the selected subset varies strongly with seed;
  \item label and token statistics estimated from 100 examples are noisy;
  \item AdaLoRA has little time to complete a meaningful allocation schedule.
\end{itemize}

A better extreme-low-resource design might freeze more components, train longer with early
stopping, use stratified sampling, initialize from an instruction-tuned classifier, or disable
dynamic allocation below a minimum step threshold. A method-selection policy could decide that
fixed LoRA is preferable when $T$ is too small for reliable adaptive allocation.

\section{Efficiency and the Adapter-Surface Problem}

Star-LoRA's current rank formula includes no explicit compute constraint. A dataset can trigger
seven module families, and even a moderate rank across all of them creates substantial work.
The runtime results show that module selection may matter more than a one- or two-unit rank change.

A compute-aware policy could solve
\begin{equation}
  \mathop{\rm maximize}_{\pi}\quad \widehat{U}(\pi,z)
  \quad \text{subject to}\quad
  C(\pi)\le C_{\max},
\end{equation}
where $\widehat{U}$ estimates task utility and $C$ estimates parameters, FLOPs, memory, or latency.
Even a simple greedy module selection under a parameter budget would make comparisons with
AdaLoRA fairer. The layer-wise results provide preliminary evidence that a smaller adapter surface
can save meaningful time, although the present heuristic loses accuracy.

\section{Interpretation of the LLM-Assisted Attempt}

The LLM experiment is valuable as a documented negative result. An LLM can reason about broad
architectural roles: lower layers may capture general patterns, upper layers may be more
task-specific, and attention and MLP projections serve different functions. However, a text prompt
does not provide the actual gradients, activation statistics, or validation loss changes needed to
identify task-critical modules.

The Claude-generated layout reduced runtime, implying that it proposed a sparser adaptation
surface. Yet its lower accuracy shows that plausible architectural reasoning is not enough.
A more promising LLM-assisted system would place the LLM inside a measured feedback loop:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item generate several candidate policies;
  \item run a short calibration phase;
  \item return loss, gradient, and cost summaries;
  \item revise the policy or select the best candidate.
\end{enumerate}
Such a system would be more expensive and should be compared with ordinary hyperparameter
optimization. It would also require exact recording of prompts, model versions, responses, and API
errors.

The failed Gemini attempts reveal another practical limitation. External APIs can be unavailable
because of quotas or rate limits. A robust experiment must distinguish the requested policy source
from the actual source used after fallback. The metadata did so correctly, but aggregate method
names did not make the fallback obvious.

\section{Required Ablations}

The following experiments are the highest priority before a publication-strength claim:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Rank only:} dataset-aware rank with fixed \texttt{q\_proj}/\texttt{v\_proj}.
  \item \textbf{Modules only:} fixed rank 8 with dataset-aware target modules.
  \item \textbf{Schedule only:} standard rank and modules with the Star-LoRA schedule.
  \item \textbf{Regularization only:} fixed rank and modules with adaptive dropout and scaling.
  \item \textbf{Rank oracle:} grid search ranks 2, 4, 8, 16, and 32 for each budget.
  \item \textbf{Budget matched:} compare methods with equal trainable parameter counts.
  \item \textbf{Stability gate:} replace or modify PEFT importance using the stable score.
\end{enumerate}

The rank-oracle comparison is especially important. If the selected Star-LoRA rank is close to the
best grid-search rank, the profiler reduces tuning cost. If not, the profile may still be useful,
but the rank mapping requires redesign.

\section{Limitations}

\subsection{Single Dataset and Model}

SST-2 is a balanced binary sentiment task. Dataset statistics that work there may not transfer to
topic classification, natural language inference, question answering, or generation. Qwen-specific
module rules also require adaptation for other architectures.

\subsection{Heuristic Coefficients}

The rank formula and thresholds are manually chosen. The numerical scales of token skew and
embedding variance are not calibrated across tokenizers and model families. A learned or
normalized controller may generalize better.

\subsection{Incomplete Experiment Metadata}

Archived results do not store complete command-line arguments, package versions, GPU type, or git
commit. This limits exact reconstruction and prevents the secondary sweep from serving as a clean
learning-rate ablation.

\subsection{No Statistical Significance Test}

Three or five seeds are useful but limited. Paired tests would require aligned seeds and clearly
matched configurations. Confidence intervals or bootstrap analysis should accompany larger
experiments.

\subsection{Stability Monitoring Only}

The current stable importance score is observational. Any statement that Star-LoRA's allocation is
stability-gated would be inaccurate. The thesis instead treats the monitor as instrumentation for
future development.

\subsection{Profiling Cost and Data Leakage}

The profiler uses only the training subset, so it does not directly leak validation labels.
Nevertheless, embedding computation adds preprocessing cost. If policies are repeatedly adjusted
after observing validation performance, ordinary model-selection leakage can occur and must be
controlled with a held-out test set.

\section{Ethical and Practical Considerations}

PEFT can reduce the resources needed to adapt language models, potentially broadening access.
However, lower adaptation cost also makes it easier to deploy models without sufficient evaluation.
Dataset-aware automation should not conceal bias or poor label quality behind a single rank score.
The profiler's class imbalance and token statistics are diagnostic signals, not a substitute for
dataset documentation.

External LLM APIs introduce privacy and reproducibility concerns. Sending dataset summaries or
examples to a provider may violate data governance rules. Model versions, prompts, and responses
must be recorded. For sensitive data, a local policy model or deterministic heuristic is safer.
